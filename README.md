# FPS Prototype (Godot 4.7)

Greybox FPS prototype: Apex Legends-style movement with CS-style weapons, on a firing range (the default map) and a greybox of Dust II.

## Maps
- **Dust II:** built from CS2's own de_dust2 navigation mesh (the walkable areas bots path on, with real floor heights), so the layout, the floor levels and the slopes match the real map: T spawn down Suicide to Mid, Long Doors and Long up to A, Pit, Short, Mid Doors, CT, the B tunnels up into B. Uneven sand is evened out into flat floors; steep stair areas are rebuilt as real steps. Real crates and ledges are there to climb. 20 target dummies stand on the sites and main angles. Buy zones at both spawns (guns come from the buy menu here; the weapon rack is only in the firing range).
- **Firing Range** (default): the hub, compact (about 60 x 76 m) and built with height. Generated by `tools/build_range.gd` (rerun it after changing the layout there; it overwrites the scene).
  - **Hub** (south): a **lounge** under the overlook (a fireplace that's always lit, armchairs, a rug, two beds), a **kitchen** on the east side (counter, stove, sink, fridge, a dining table with chairs), spawn, the **EXPEDITION** terminal, the **ENEMIES** lever and the **SUPPLIES** crate just ahead of spawn; the **armoury** on the west wall; bleachers along the south wall, benches, a balcony over the firing line and an overlook platform in the south-west corner (stairs up both).
  - **Range** (middle): five lanes, left to right: lane 3 (the spray wall), lane 2 (20 m), lane 1 (10 m), lane 4 (30 m), lane 5 (40 m); you can walk along the tops of its side walls.
  - **Yard** (west): scaffolding with a deck at 3 m and a top deck at 6 m (stairs, then a ramp), crates and benches.
  - **Course** (east): slide ramp and slide strip, 3 / 5 / 7 m gap jumps, crouch tunnel, mantle blocks, climb walls (3.6 m you can make, 4.6 m you can't) and a tower.
  - **Ziplines:** overlook to the low deck, top deck over the range to the course tower, balcony to the slide ramp tower.
  - **Time of day:** the **clock** by the terminals (E) steps it on for everyone: morning, noon, evening, night. The sun (a pale moon at night), sky and ambient light fade over 2.5 s, and the **lamp posts** all round the map and on the platforms light up for the evening and night. `scripts/hub.gd` has the presets.
  - **Bedroom:** through the doorways either end of the bleachers, a room behind the south wall: four beds in two pairs along the back wall, each pair with a nightstand, lamp and window, wardrobes between them, a reading corner (armchairs, side table, floor lamp), a desk and a bookshelf, plants, a big rug and pictures on the walls.
  - **Scenery** (just for looks, you can't get to it): grass to the horizon, a ring of snow-capped mountains that fade to blue in the haze, apartment blocks out past the walls (their windows light up at night) and pines scattered round the walls, three sparse rows, well spaced so the buildings and mountains still show between and over them.
  - **Signs fold down while you aim:** every standing sign (lanes, distances, course labels) tips flat when you ADS, so it never blocks your view, and stands back up when you stop.
- **Research Complex:** a generated expedition (see Expeditions below). Start it from the terminal, or Esc → Maps (a new layout every time).
- **Esc → Maps** switches map. Online, only the host can, and everyone goes with them.
- **M opens the full map:** the whole level north up, with the fog of war, everyone's position and facing (with names), and markers for the entrance, breakers and extraction once you've uncovered them. M again closes it, and you can keep moving while it's open.
- **Spawns:** each map has a `PlayerSpawn` marker (a Marker3D in the `player_spawns` group). Players line up in rows beside and behind it. Add more markers to place spawns by hand.
- **Radar** (top-left): rotates with you so your facing is always up, with target dummies as red dots, teammates as dots in their colour, and A/B on Dust II. It's drawn when the level loads, from the level's collision boxes seen from above, so any map gets one.
- Dust II is generated by `tools/build_dust2.gd` from `tools/data/dust2.txt` (floor areas, walls, spawns, targets and the radar picture, in metres). That data was converted from the nav mesh in awpy's test data (`tests/de_dust2.json` in pnxenopoulos/awpy); it's derived from Valve's map, so keep this project private rather than distributing it.
- **Moving around:** you walk up steps and ledges up to 0.45 m (Source's 18 units) without jumping, and slopes up to 52° (stairs).

## Open it
1. Install **Godot 4.7+** (standard build, not .NET). On Windows: `winget install GodotEngine.GodotEngine`.
2. Godot Project Manager → **Import** → pick `project.godot`.
3. Press **F5** to play.

## Multiplayer (co-op, 1–10 players)
- **The game opens in the Firing Range**, offline. Host or join from there.
- **Esc → Lobby:** set your name, then one player presses **Host**. The others type the host's address and press **Join**. Joining drops you into the host's map.
- **The lobby shows all 10 slots:** colour, P1–P10 (P1 is the host), name, HOST/YOU tags, and each player's health, DOWNED or OUT; empty slots say OPEN. In game, the inventory (**Tab**) shows your squad's shields and health.
- **Test on one PC:** in the editor, Debug → Customize Run Instances → enable it, set 2+ instances, then F5. Host in one window, join `127.0.0.1` in the other. From a terminal: `godot --path . -- --host` and `godot --path . -- --join=127.0.0.1`. Add `--name=<name>` to give each copy its own name (not saved), and `--port=<n>` for another port (default 24680).
- **Over the internet**, the host must forward UDP port 24680, or use a VPN such as Tailscale or ZeroTier. Steam lobbies and invites will replace this later.
- **The game doesn't pause online.** Esc opens the menu, and you stand still while it's open.
- **What's shared:**
  - everyone's movement (smoothed, about 30 updates a second), crouching, and where they're looking
  - their shots: tracers, impacts and gunshots, quieter with distance
  - health, downed, revives, and who's out
  - enemies
  - target dummies' shield and health bars
- **Damage numbers** only show for the player who did the damage. Health bars are the same for everyone.
- **Other players** are coloured capsules with their name and a health bar overhead. When they're down, they lie on the ground and their tag (visible through walls) shows their bleed-out time or revive progress.
- **Not shared yet** (each player sees their own copy): the weapon rack, dropped and thrown guns, knife swings, reload sounds.
- **How it works:**
  - `Net` (`scripts/net/net.gd`) owns the connection. It uses ENet for now, and Steam will only replace the code there.
  - `Game` (`scripts/net/game.gd`) holds the players under `Game/Players`, outside the level, so they survive map changes. The host spawns a `Player` per peer (named after its peer id) and picks the map.
  - Each machine simulates its own player; that's client-authoritative movement, so it feels the same as offline. Everyone else's copy follows the synced values (see the Networking section of `player.gd`).
  - The host decides all health (`PlayerHealth`, enemies, dummies). When you hit something, your game shows the hit straight away and tells the host, and the host sends everyone the result.
  - **Physics layers:**
    - 1 world: geometry and target dummies
    - 2 pickups
    - 3 players: bullets pass through teammates
    - 4 enemies
    - 5 doors: the breach door. It blocks players, enemies and bullets, but navigation is baked as if it weren't there.
    - 6 props: crates and bodies you can push and shoot around (see Physics props).

## Expeditions (Research Complex)
- **Start one:** use the **EXPEDITION** terminal in the hub (anyone can), or the host can pick Esc → Maps → Research Complex. Everyone goes in together.
- **The goal:**
  1. **Get in.** You arrive outside, in the yard, and the only way into the facility is the striped **breach door**. Either:
     - shoot or hit it down (400 health, a shared bar), or
     - **plant a breach charge** on it (E). It blows 3 s later and hurts anyone within 5 m (up to 45).
     - Either way it's loud and wakes what's inside.
  2. Find the **breakers** in the objective rooms and **restore power** at each one (E).
  3. Find the **extraction console** at the far end and start it.
  4. **Get on the blue pad** before the 25 s countdown ends, while enemies pour in from the back of that room.
  - The objective is shown at the top of the screen.
- **Fog of war:** the radar and the full map (M) start dark. Each room appears whole the moment anyone in the squad steps into it, and everyone shares what's been found.
- **Heal items** (syringes, medkits, the odd trauma kit, shield cells and batteries) and **ammo boxes** are lying around at some of the rooms' supply spots: on benches, desks, platforms (E to take one). See Health below.
- **When the countdown ends:** whoever's on the pad is **extracted**, even if downed; anyone else is **left behind**. Either way, after the results everyone is back in the hub.
  - Extraction model A: extracting ends this expedition. Rewards and losing them come next, in Phase 4.
- **A squad wipe** ends the expedition too, and everyone goes back to the hub.
- **Breakers are loud:** throwing one wakes any enemies within 30 m.
- **Enemies** (brawlers, see Enemies below):
  - A room's enemies appear, standing idle, when someone gets within about one room of it.
  - Idle ones wake up when they see you, when you get close (6 m, or 1.5 m if you're crouched), or when they're shot, and they wake the others within 15 m. Crouch-walk up behind one for a **backstab**: melee from behind does double damage (the big gold number).
  - How many: 2, plus 1 for every 2 rooms deep, plus 1 per 2 players, up to the room's limit (lab 8, atrium 12, ...). Corridors are sometimes empty.
  - The extraction brings 3 waves of 3 + one per player.
- **The layout:**
  - The host picks a random seed, and every player builds the same map from it, late joiners included.
  - It's a tree grown from the yard: a main path of corridors and combat rooms, 2 objective rooms branching off it, the extraction room at the end, and a couple of side branches. So everything can be reached.
  - Doorways that happen to face each other are joined too, making loops; unused ones are sealed.
  - Planning is in `scripts/expedition/layout.gd`; building it, the objectives and the encounters are in `expedition.gd`.
- **Rooms:** handcrafted modules in `scenes/rooms/research/`:
  - start: the yard (outside, 1×2 cells, no roof) with the breach door
  - corridors: airlock, corridor, corner, junction
  - combat: lab, office, and a 2×2 atrium with a raised platform, ramps and a catwalk
  - objectives: server room and generator room (each has a breaker)
  - extraction (2 cells)
- **How rooms fit together:**
  - They sit on a **24 m grid** with 6 m walls, and doorways are **4.4 × 3.6 m** in the middle of a cell edge, so any two rooms fit and a squad can move through together.
  - On each room: its `Doors` markers (facing out), `EnemySpawns` markers and `Supplies` markers (possible heal item spots), and `kind` / `cells` / `max_enemies` / `weight` on the root (`scripts/expedition/room.gd`).
- **Edit or add rooms** by hand in the editor:
  - Keep floors at y = 0 and everything inside the footprint, and put doorways only on cell-edge midpoints.
  - New rooms go in `ROOMS` in `expedition.gd`.
  - The current set was generated by `tools/build_rooms.gd` (`godot --headless -s tools/build_rooms.gd`). Rerunning it overwrites the rooms it lists, so take out any you've edited by hand.
- **Greybox for now:** rooms inside have a ceiling and a light per cell; the yard is open to the sky. Apart from the breach door, no doors open or lock yet.
- **Fallback:** if you ever fall out of a map, you're put back at a spawn point.

## Health, downed and revives
- **Health:** 100. Your stats sit bottom left, Apex style: the health number (big; red when low), the shield bar over the health bar, your self-revive kits (a yellow **+** and how many) under them and, in its own box to the right of the bars, the heal item you last picked on the wheel (its icon, how many, and the heal key, [4]). The rest are in the inventory. A red flash at the screen edges and arcs around the crosshair show where hits come from.
- **Healing:**
  - **Regeneration:** slow. After 12 s without taking damage, health trickles back at 1 a second (a minute and a half from empty).
  - **Heal items:** **hold 4** for the heal wheel, point the mouse at an item and let go, or click one in the inventory (Tab). The wheel is laid out like Apex's, clockwise from the top: trauma kit, shield battery, shield cell, stim, medkit; with nothing picked the middle says CANCEL. A quick **tap of 4** uses your last pick, or the smallest that helps (shields first if yours is dented).
    | | Restores | Takes | Carry | Start with |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | Syringe | 25 | 2 s | 4 | 2 |
    | Medkit | 60 | 4 s | 2 | 1 |
    | Trauma Kit | full health and shield | 7 s | 1 | 0 |
    | Shield Cell | 25 shield | 1.25 s | 4 | 2 |
    | Shield Battery | full shield | 2.5 s | 2 | 0 |
  - **No holding:** once picked, it applies itself, and you see your own arms do it while the gun drops out of view: a **syringe** winds a bandage round your forearm; a **medkit** or **trauma kit** is held in both hands, its lid flips open and you keep dipping in for supplies; a **shield cell** or **battery** is pulled apart in both hands (the charge glowing between the halves), then pushed together and pressed to your chest.
  - **While it applies** you walk slowly and can't shoot.
  - **Cancelling:** shooting, aiming, sprinting, meleeing, reloading, switching, throwing, using something or pressing 4 again cancels it, and you keep the item.
  - **The reminder:** "HOLD 4 HEAL" shows at 25 health or less, when you have something to use.
  - More heal items turn up on expeditions. Every map (re)start restocks you to the starting amounts.
- **Shields (Apex style):** 100 of armour on top of your health: purple tier, the purple bar above it (in chunks of 25; the colour follows Apex tiers: white 50, blue 75, purple 100, red 125). Damage takes the shield first; it flashes and clangs when hit and shatters when it breaks. Teammates' shields show over their heads and in the inventory.
  - **They never recharge by themselves.** Top them up with shield cells (+25) and batteries (full), from the heal wheel or the inventory.
  - You start every map with a full shield. Going down loses it: you get back up with none.
- **At 0 you're downed:** you crawl, can't use weapons, a red vignette pulses round the screen, the colour fades to 25%, and you bleed out after **30 s**.
  - **Revive:** a teammate holds **E** next to you for **5 s**.
  - **Self-revive kit:** hold **E** yourself for **5 s**; the camera pulls out to third person while you do. Everyone starts with **1**, restocked when the map (re)starts.
  - While a revive is happening, the bleed-out pauses and neither player can move. Letting go of E, or the reviver getting downed or moving away, cancels it and loses the progress.
  - You get back up with 30 health.
- **Bleeding out** takes you out of the run: you spectate a teammate (fire switches who) and come back when the map restarts.
- **Squad wipe:** when nobody is standing and nobody downed has a kit, the map restarts after 4 s, with everyone back at full health.
- **Spawn protection:** you can't take damage for 1 s after a (re)start.
- **Tuning:** all the numbers are on the `Health` node in `scenes/player.tscn`; crawl speed and downed height are on `Player`.
- **Supply crate:** the white **SUPPLIES** crate in the Firing Range (right of the enemy lever) fills your pouch with every heal and shield item (E). It never runs out.
- **Hazard:** the red **HAZARD** pad in the Firing Range (left, in front of spawn) does 25 damage a second. Use it to test going down. Drop a `scripts/hazard_zone.gd` Area3D into any map for more.

## Enemies
- **The ENEMIES lever** just ahead of the Firing Range spawn turns them on and off for everyone (look at it and press **E**). Anyone can pull it. Every map starts with enemies off, including after a squad wipe.
- **Brawlers** (purple, melee) are the only enemy in use for now. In the Firing Range they spawn at the back of the yard and the course: up to 5 at a time from each of 2 spawners, with a replacement 3 s after one dies.
- **How brawlers fight:**
  - They run (6.5 m/s) at the nearest player who's still standing: faster than you run, slower than you sprint.
  - The moment one touches you, you're hit for 34 (three hits down a full-health player) and knocked back 8 m/s, with your camera shaken. Then it needs 0.8 s before it can hit again, but it keeps coming.
  - They barge crates and bodies out of their way.
- **Killed enemies leave a body:** a physics corpse thrown well away from whoever killed it (6–20 m/s, harder for a harder killing hit). See Physics props.
- **Bodies:** enemies and other players are blocky people with legs and arms that walk as they move. Brawlers reach for you with claws; grunts and players hold their guns up.
- **Grunts** (orange, the gun version) are kept for later: `scenes/enemies/grunt.tscn`.
  - When they can see you within 22 m, they stop, wind up for 0.6 s, then fire a 3-shot burst (7 damage a shot), and close in to about 10 m.
  - Use them by setting a spawner's `enemy_scene`, or `ENEMY` in `expedition.gd`.
- **They ignore downed players**, so a revive is risky rather than hopeless. They turn on whoever shot them last. Headshots count. Damage numbers and the bar work like the dummies.
- **The host runs them**, on a navigation mesh baked from the map's collision when a map with enemy spawners loads.
- **Add them to a map:** add a `scripts/enemies/enemy_spawner.gd` Marker3D (facing where they should look) and set `max_alive` and `respawn_delay`.
- **Tuning:** on `scenes/enemies/brawler.tscn` (the Melee group: contact range, damage, knockback, recovery; plus speed) and `grunt.tscn` (speed, range, wind-up, burst, damage, spread).

## Physics props (crates and bodies)
- **What they are:** crates in the Firing Range and the expedition rooms, plus **bodies**: every killed enemy, and any player who bleeds out, leaves one.
- **What moves them:**
  - **Walk into one** to push it. One player can shove anything up to about 60 kg along at walking pace; the big Firing Range crate (90 kg) takes two.
  - **Bullets and knife hits** knock them about (harder for harder-hitting guns).
  - **Brawlers** barge them aside, and the breach charge blasts them away.
- **Climbing:** you can mantle onto crates.
- **How it's networked:** the host simulates them and everyone sees the same thing. On a client your pushes go to the host, so there's a moment's delay.
- **Clean-up:** bodies stay 45 s, with 30 at most (the oldest go first).
- **Add crates to a map:** place a `scripts/physics/prop_spawn.gd` Marker3D (bottom centre of the crate) and set its `size`, `color` and `mass`.

## Knockback
- **Big hits throw you:** a brawler's hit (8 m/s) or a breach charge going off next to you (up to 16 m/s) throws you away from it and shakes your camera. Your aim doesn't move.
- **Even the hit that downs you** sends you flying.
- Dust II has no enemies yet.

## Inventory and ammo
- **Tab opens the inventory** (Tab or Esc closes it). You can keep moving, but not look around or shoot. Two pages, picked at the top:
  - **Inventory:** your weapons on top, your backpack under them.
    - **Weapons:** your two guns side by side (name, ammo type, rounds in the mag and spare) and your knife. **Drag one gun onto the other** (or press ⇄) to swap their slots; **right-click a gun** (or DROP) to drop it. The one in your hands has a green outline.
    - **Backpack:** your heals and shields (click one to use it), your self-revive kits, and your spare ammo.
  - **Squad:** the lobby board: everyone's name, shield and health, or DOWNED / OUT. It's the only page while you're down or out.
- **Ammo is shared by type**, like Apex: every gun taking the same type draws on the same spare rounds. Weapon names are all white; **the ammo count is underlined in its ammo type's colour**: light amber, heavy teal, shotgun red, sniper blue.
  | Type | Guns | Carry | Start an expedition with | Box |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Light | Kestrel, Hornet, Vanta | 240 | 120 | 60 |
  | Heavy | Desert Eagle, R8 Revolver, M4A4, AK-47, Negev | 180 | 90 | 45 |
  | Shotgun | Breacher, Olympia | 40 | 16 | 10 |
  | Sniper | Heron, Harrier, SSG-008 | 40 | 20 | 10 |
- **Unlimited in the hub.** On an expedition you start with the amounts above (and full mags) and find **ammo boxes** at supply spots: E takes one if you have room. Dropped guns keep what's in their mag; spare rounds stay with you.
- Tuning: `AMMO` at the top of `scripts/weapons/weapon_manager.gd`; each gun's type is `ammo_type` in its `.tres`.

## Weapons
You carry **two guns and a knife**. You start with the Vanta and Kestrel. Every gun is in the **armoury** on the west wall, on your right as you spawn: long guns on a two-level rack, pistols and the SMG lying on the table in front, lit up at night (they restock after 3 s). Look at one for its name: look at one and press E to pick it up, or swap it for the gun in your hands when both slots are full. **Walking over a dropped gun picks it up** if you have a free slot (it goes in the slot without switching to it; guns you just threw or dropped wait 1 s).

| | Style | Fire | Damage (head) | Mag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kestrel | Light, quick pistol, extended mag | Semi 480 rpm, or a 3-round burst (X) | 17 (34) | 20 |
| Desert Eagle | CS2 Deagle, spin draw | Semi, 267 rpm | 53 (106) | 7 |
| R8 Revolver | CS2 R8: hold fire to cock | Fires 0.32 s into the hold, 110 rpm | 72 (144) | 8 |
| Hornet | R-99-ish | Auto, 1000 rpm | 11 (16.5) | 22 |
| Breacher | Titanfall / Apex Mastiff-ish | Pump, 70 rpm, 8 pellets in a flat line | 13 per pellet (19.5) | 6, shell by shell |
| M4A4 | CS M4A4 | Auto, 666 rpm | 15 (30) | 30 |
| AK-47 | CS AK-47: harder kick | Auto, 600 rpm | 19 (38) | 30 |
| Vanta | R-301-ish AR | Auto, 780 rpm | 14 (24.5) | 28 |
| Heron | Longbow-ish, holo optic (2.2x) | Semi, 240 rpm | 38 (76) | 10 |
| Harrier | CS:GO AWP look-alike (olive-green thumbhole chassis, folded bipod, big brake and scope), 7x scope | Bolt action, 40 rpm | 135 (270) | 5 |
| SSG-008 | CS2 SSG 08 look-alike (skeleton stock, fluted barrel), 4x scope | Bolt action, 48 rpm | 88 (220) | 10 |
| Knife | Always carried | Light slash / heavy stab (2x from behind an enemy or range dummy) | 30 / 60 | — |
| Olympia | Over-under double barrel | Two blasts back to back, 10 pellets | 14 per pellet (21) | 2 |
| Negev | CS2 Negev-ish light machine gun | Auto, 780 rpm | 13 (26) | 60 |

- **Breacher (shotgun):** modelled on Titanfall's Mastiff, and like Apex's Mastiff its 8 pellets fly out in a **flat horizontal line**, evenly spaced, 5° either side from the hip and 2.2° aimed (the crosshair opens sideways only). Other shotguns still spread in a ring. Full damage out to 10 m, falling off to 26 m. A blast that hits a target counts as one hit. It pumps after every shot and **reloads one shell at a time**: pull the trigger mid-reload and it finishes the shell going in, closes up, then fires.
- **R8 Revolver:** hold fire and the hammer cocks; the shot goes when it's back, and it keeps firing while you hold. Let go early and nothing fires. Pinpoint accurate. The cylinder swings out to reload.
- **Desert Eagle:** the draw tosses it up spinning around the barrel; first shot is accurate, spamming isn't.
- **Muzzle flash** shrinks to 30% while aimed (`ads_flash_scale`), so it doesn't block the sight picture.
- **Harrier (sniper):** wildly inaccurate from the hip, dead on when scoped (the screen goes to a full scope), but moving while scoped throws shots off. After each shot it drops out of the scope to work the bolt, then scopes back in if you're still aiming (AWP style).
- **Inspect:** hold F to keep it looping; R stops it (and reloads a gun that needs it). Knives can inspect mid-draw (the draw blends into the inspect); pressing F during an inspect lets it carry on. The draw time still has to pass before you can slash.
- **Ammo goes in the moment the magazine does** (the mag-in sound in each reload animation), so a quick swap straight after keeps the full mag.
- Switching away cuts off the old weapon's draw and reload sounds.
- **Every weapon handles differently by ear:** each gun has its own recorded mag, slide, bolt and pump sounds (pitched lower for the big ones), and each knife its own ring of steel on the draw (and nothing else after it), a sharp slash on the swing and a bite on the hit (`_voices_setup()` in `scripts/sfx.gd`).
- **Your hands do the work:** each hand is on its own pivot, and the hand work is worked out from what the gun parts do. Reloading, your left hand reaches down to the magazine, pulls it out and off screen, comes back with a fresh one, pushes it home and slaps it, then goes back to the handguard. Racking a slide or pulling a charging handle (draws and empty reloads), the left hand goes up and pulls it. On a bolt-action (Harrier, SSG-008) your right hand leaves the grip to work the bolt. On the pump it rides the pump. When an inspect knocks the magazine, the left hand taps it. (`_auto_hands()` in `tools/build_weapons.gd`; a clip that keys a hand itself keeps its own.)
- **Cocking turns the gun to your hand:** while the left hand pulls a charging handle or slide, the gun rolls so the handle faces it, and the hand takes it from well outside the gun (6 cm out) and travels round the outside of the gun there and back. A handle on top (the M4's) is turned up and to the left, about 25 degrees. A handle on the right side gets one of two moves: the AK and Negev roll counter-clockwise (75 and 85 degrees, like CS2's AK draw) while also pulling in and up with the muzzle swinging round, then back the other way through the pull, so they travel rather than spin in place; the hand reaches over the top and takes hold of the handle; the Vanta and DMR roll clockwise (70 degrees) so the handle faces down to the hand coming up from below. Either way the arm never crosses the gun. (Checked with the real arm solver: no arm through the receiver on any of them, bar a 1-2 cm graze on the Vanta and DMR on the way.)
- **Hands stay on the arms:** each hand turns to meet its forearm wherever an animation takes it (over the top of a gun, down to a magazine), so the wrist always faces the elbow and the hand never looks broken off the arm.
- **Each gun has its own feel** (`GUN_STYLE` in `tools/build_weapons.gd`): how far it rolls when cocking, its move as the magazine goes home (the AK rocks it in, the SMG cants, the DMR dips), where the palm slaps the magazine, and how hard the reload cues kick the camera (the Negev and AK hardest, the SMG and pistols lightest).
- **CS2-style inspects** for the rifles, SMG, DMR, Negev and both bolt-actions: the gun comes up and turns to show its left side, holds there with a slow drift, rolls over to show the right side and top, then settles back. Each gun turns its own way and at its own pace, with its own beat: the M4 and Negev tap the magazine, the SMG, AK and Harrier pull the magazine and seat it again, the Vanta, DMR and SSG-008 ease the bolt or charging handle back (a chamber check). The pistols and shotguns keep their own inspects.
- **The two bolt-actions handle differently:** the Harrier is heavy and deliberate: its draw follows CS:GO's AWP, bolted as it's lifted: your right hand's on the bolt from the start and the rifle comes up from low right already leant left (bolt turned up to you), the bolt going up and back on the way, slamming home with a jolt as it reaches the shoulder, then it rolls level onto target; after each shot (and an empty reload) it leans over and runs the bolt the same way; its inspect is slow and high with a mag check. The SSG-008 is light and quick: it sweeps in from low left, and the muzzle just tips up for a fast flick of the bolt, so it stays on target between shots; it reloads tipped up and swung left rather than rolled over, and its inspect is a fast flick right over onto its side.
- **Reloads kick the camera** a little on mag out, mag in and the slide/bolt (`RELOAD_PUNCH` in `scripts/weapons/viewmodel_rig.gd`).
- **Switching is instant** (CS style): the new weapon's draw starts straight away, with no lowering the old one first. Turn on `stow_on_switch` on the `Weapons` node in `player.tscn` to bring the lowering back.
- **Q switches to your last weapon.** Q Q mid-reload, once the mag is in, skips the rest of the animation: you're ready after one draw and keep the ammo. Q Q before the mag goes in cancels the reload.

- **G throws your gun.** It spins end over end, hits a target for 20–30 damage (x2 on the head), and lands as a pickup with its ammo intact. You switch to your other gun, or the knife.
- **Buy menu (B):** works inside the green buy zones (spawn, and the movement course hub). CS:GO-style radial: pick a category (Pistols, SMGs, Heavy, Rifles, Knives), then an item. Point with the mouse or press its number, click to buy; right-click goes back, B or Esc closes. You can keep moving while it's open. Everything is free. Buying a gun you own refills it; otherwise it fills a free slot or replaces the gun in your hands (the old one drops).
- **Knives** are held CS2-style: blade angled up and across the screen with the flat towards you, free hand low on the left (it drops out of view while you inspect). The grip is `_knife_grip()` in `tools/build_weapons.gd`.
- **Knives** come from the buy menu (one Knives wheel). The last one you bought is saved for next time. Every knife cuts the same; each has its own look, draw, inspect (F) and quick-draw flair:
- **Knife points:** every blade is a straight run off the centre of its handle into a tapered point the same height and thickness as the blade: level with the spine for the drop points (combat knife, bayonet, M9, butterfly), lower for the Bowie's clip point, on the centreline for the stiletto, kunai and daggers.
  - **Combat Knife:** tossed up barrel-rolling around the blade.
  - **Bayonet:** long blade; end-over-end flip toss.
  - **M9 Bayonet:** CS2-style: spins around your index finger at the guard. Draw twirls it forward round the finger; inspect shows the flat, rolls it to show the saw back, twirls it twice, drops to a reverse grip and snaps back.
  - **Bowie Knife:** big clip-point blade with slow, heavy moves: drawn in a reverse grip and tossed into a turn and a half; inspect sights down the edge, rolls it over, flips to an ice-pick grip and jabs, then tosses it back.
  - **Karambit:** hooked claw blade honed to a needle point, black handle and ring, held across the fist with the ring out to the right and the whole blade out to the left, its hooked blade built as one joined curve, held a little further out in front than the other knives, pointing well away from you and tipped down so the point sits just below level on screen, hooking upward, its flat to you; spun like the kunai (blade left, a turn and a half in the hand when you sprint).
  - **Butterfly Knife:** black handles; the most twirling of any knife, and quick with it: the draw snaps the blade open in an aerial with the bite handle whipping round twice, the inspect chains six turns of the blade (aerials, rollovers, fans, a zen loop with both handles), its slashes and heavy stab finish with a slow, lazy twirl of the handle and blade that plays on after the attack (you can attack again as soon as ever; a new cut just takes over), and even the sprint switch flips the handles round. Its tricks make only the snapping sounds, no whooshes; every twirl after an attack or a sprint switch snaps softly.
  - **Stiletto Knife:** switchblade; the blade pops open on the button, and inspect closes and pops it twice.
  - **Shadow Daggers:** a push dagger in each fist; both come up and twirl, and the free hand stays up for the inspect.
  - **Kunai** (after Wraith's heirloom): a leaf blade with a glowing purple fuller and a ring pommel; it spins round your finger by the ring. Held Apex style, blade pointing left across the screen; start sprinting and it spins a turn and a half in your hand (like its draw, slower) and is caught with the loop pointing left and the blade back behind your fist; stop and it spins back to the blade-left hold (its own `sprint` / `sprint_end` animations). Draws and inspects spin it in your hand.
- **Knives ease into sprinting:** the fist drops into the run while the knife spins once in your hand, and spins again on the way back up when you stop (the kunai does its own turn-and-a-half twirl, caught blade out to the right; the karambit is quicker (0.5 s): it turns to point straight away from you, twirls once end over end in that straight line (point up and away over the top) with the wrist flicking along, then turns into its new hold, which for the run is swung a little further, out to the right and away from you, its hook curving away so the point faces out ahead, not down).
- **V is a quick knife slash** from any gun: the gun ducks away and the knife cuts in from the side.
- **Draws:** guns always play their full flourish (turn off `always_flourish_guns` on the `Weapons` node in `player.tscn` to have them alternate with a quick raise again); knives always play their full flourish too, except the M9 Bayonet, which alternates with a quick draw (`alternate_draws` in a knife's `.tres`). Flourishes: the Vanta spins up and racks its charging handle, the Hornet gets tossed into a flip, the Kestrel does a revolver twirl, each knife has its own (below). You can shoot once the whole draw animation has played (and never sooner than `draw_time`). Any animation in a weapon's scene whose name starts with `draw` joins the rotation. A freshly picked-up or bought gun starts with its flourish.
- **Weapon list (CS2 style):** the ammo count sits at the bottom right with your weapons stacked under it, each as its name and key (1 / 2 / 3) in white; the one in your hands is bigger and lit, the others dimmed. The mag count is underlined in its ammo type's colour.
- **What's in your hands changes your speed (sprint included):** rifles and SMGs 1.0x, Kestrel 1.06x, knife 1.12x (`move_speed` in each `.tres`).

- **Recoil is Apex-style:** each gun kicks your view in a fixed pattern you learn to pull down against. ADS shots go exactly where the crosshair is; hipfire has spread (the crosshair gap shows it). Recoil you didn't pull down drifts back after you stop firing (set `recoil_recovery` to 0 for pure Apex).
- Firing or aiming stops a sprint, and there's a short sprint-to-fire delay.
- **Fire modes (X):** guns with a burst mode switch between it and their normal mode with X. The Kestrel's burst fires 3 rounds at 1100 rpm per pull. The HUD shows the mode next to the ammo.
- **Stand still or crouch to shoot straighter:** standing still, hipfire spread and bloom shrink by 90% for every gun except scoped rifles (the Harrier and SSG-008: 20%); crouched, by 30%, which stacks (down to 10% at most). `crouch_accuracy` / `still_accuracy` / `still_accuracy_scoped` on the `Weapons` node.
- **Switching to a gun rattles its sling** (knives come out quietly: just their draw).
- **No shooting until a draw or reload has completely finished.** Empty mag auto-reloads. Switching weapons before the mag goes in cancels the reload; after it, Q Q skips the rest of the animation (you keep the ammo, then wait out the draw).
- Melee works mid-reload. Inspect cancels on fire, aim, reload or switch; R eases the weapon back from wherever the inspect had it (0.35 s) instead of snapping. **Hold F as you switch to the knife** and it comes out straight into its inspect (the draw's ring of steel still plays right away).
- Targets have 100 shield (purple) + 100 health like purple armour, show damage numbers (to the shooter), fall over when killed and refill after 3 s without damage.

## Movement tricks to test
- **Slide boost:** sprint, then crouch. You get +2.4 m/s once every 2 s.
- **Slide-hop:** jump out of a slide and keep crouch held. You'll re-slide on landing and keep your speed.
- **Bunny hop:** press jump just before landing. You skip friction on the landing tick.
- **Air strafe:** in the air, hold A or D and turn the mouse the same way.
- **Slide ramp:** slide down the orange ramp to build speed (~13–14 m/s).
- **Mantle:** jump at a ledge while moving towards it, and you pull yourself up onto it. That works for ledges 0.5–1.7 m above your feet at the time, so crates, truck beds and the atrium platform. The gun tucks away, and you can't shoot until you're up.
- **Superglide (Apex):** jump in the last 0.1 s of a mantle, or within 0.1 s of coming over the top, and you launch forward at 10 m/s with a low hop. Hold crouch to land it in a slide.
  - **The cue:** while you mantle, the crosshair is replaced by a ring closing in on a circle. Jump when they meet and it turns green; it turns red if you jumped too early.
  - Jump any earlier in the mantle and it's spoiled: you just climb up.
- **Tuning** for both is in the Mantle group on `Player`.
- **Wall climb (Apex):** every wall is climbable a little. Jump at one holding forward and you scramble up it for about 1.9 m, so ledges up to about 4.2 m above the floor come within mantle reach (you mantle the moment one does). Too high and you drop back off. The gun is tucked away while you climb.
- **Wall jump:** jump in the air with a wall right next to you (any side, climbing or not) to kick off it: 4.5 m/s away and a small hop. Two per time in the air, never off the same wall twice in a row. Tuning: the Walls group on `Player`.
- **Ziplines (Apex / Titanfall):** press **E** near the yellow cable (standing or mid-jump) to grab on; you never latch on by yourself. You ride towards whichever end you're looking at, 14 m/s up or down, and can look around and shoot. **Jump** to leap off with your speed, **crouch** or **E** to drop; at the end you're let off onto its platform. Put a `scripts/zipline.gd` node in any map (set `end` and the pole heights). Tuning: the Zipline group on `Player`.
- **Crouching under things:** you can't stand up under a ceiling, a teammate or an enemy on top of you. Every spawn starts you standing.

## Settings (Esc → Settings)
- **Mouse & View:** sensitivity on the CS2/Source scale (type in your CS2 value), ADS sensitivity multiplier (1.0 = zoom-matched), invert Y, and FOV (horizontal at 16:9; 106 = CS2).
- **Controls:** hold or toggle for crouch, sprint and aim (aim is toggle by default), plus a primary and secondary binding for every action. Click a binding, then press a key or mouse button. Esc cancels, Backspace clears. Binding a key that's already in use unbinds it from the other action.
- Settings save automatically to `user://settings.cfg` (on Windows: `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\FPS Prototype\`).
- New actions show up in the menu once you add them to `REBINDABLE` in `scripts/settings.gd`.

## Tuning
Select `Player` in `scenes/player.tscn`. Every movement value is in the Inspector, grouped by Ground, Air, Slide, Crouch and Camera FX. Viewmodel sway, bob, idle breathing and poses are on the `Viewmodel` node.

Each weapon's stats (damage, fire rate, spread, recoil pattern, ADS zoom, reload times, melee timings, viewmodel positions) are in `weapons/*.tres`. Open one in the Inspector, even while the game runs. The starting loadout, knife, infinite reserve ammo and throw settings are on the `Weapons` node in `player.tscn`.

**Animations** live in each `scenes/weapons/*.tscn`, generated by `tools/build_weapons.gd`. Their keys are smoothed and baked into dense keys, so retime or restyle them in the generator and rerun it rather than dragging baked keys in the Animation panel. `Pivot` moves the whole hand, `Pivot/Gun` moves the weapon alone (spins, tosses), plus `Magazine` and `Bolt`/`Slide`. Method keys calling `play_sfx` trigger sounds. Guns need: `idle`, `draw`, `draw_quick`, `inspect`, `reload`, `reload_empty`, `melee_lower` (plus `cycle` with `cycle_after_shot`; shell reloads use `reload_start`, `reload_shell`, `reload_end` instead of `reload`/`reload_empty`). Melee weapons need: `idle`, `draw`, `draw_quick`, `inspect`, `slash_left`, `slash_right`, `heavy`, `quick_slash`. Reloads are stretched to the weapon's reload time; the melee hit lands at the `*_hit_time` in the knife's `.tres`.

To add a gun: duplicate a `.tres` and a scene in `scenes/weapons/`, then add a `WeaponSpawner` (scripts/weapons/weapon_spawner.gd) for it in the map. To add a knife: write a `_knife_<id>()` in `tools/build_weapons.gd` (a parts list plus one of the shared draws/inspects), add it to `NEW_KNIVES` there, rerun the generator, then add it to `KNIVES` in `scripts/settings.gd` so it shows up in the buy menu. New guns go in the buy menu's categories at the top of `scripts/ui/buy_menu.gd`. To add a buy zone, drop a `BuyZone` (Area3D with scripts/buy_zone.gd) in the map and set its `size`. The butterfly's handles (`Pivot/Gun/HandleA`, `HandleB`) and the flip knife's blade (`Pivot/Gun/BladeHinge`) have their own animation tracks.
